Notice of Termination of an Employment Contract
Labour Code of the Republic of Lithuania, Article 64

Where an employer terminates an employment contract on grounds that are not related to the employee's fault, the employer must give the employee written notice in advance. The general notice period is one month. Where the employment relationship has lasted less than one year, the notice period is two weeks.

These notice periods are doubled for employees who have less than five years left until the statutory old-age pension age, and tripled for the following employees: those raising a child under fourteen years of age, those raising a disabled child under eighteen years of age, pregnant employees, employees who have less than two years left until the statutory old-age pension age, and disabled employees.

During the notice period, the employee must be granted, at their request, time off to look for a new job. This time off is at least ten percent of the standard working time over the notice period, and the employee retains their average wage for it.

If the employer fails to comply with the required notice period, the employee is entitled to compensation. When the contract is terminated on the employer's initiative without the employee's fault, the employee is generally also paid a severance payment, the size of which depends on the length of the continuous employment relationship.

An employee may also terminate an employment contract on their own initiative by giving the employer written notice, as a rule twenty calendar days in advance, or in certain cases established by law a shorter or longer period.
